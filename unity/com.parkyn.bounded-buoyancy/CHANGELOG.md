## [2.5.0] - 2026-10-15

- Lines: **Bounded Buoyancy Line**, a line that pulls but never pushes (a fishing line, a mooring rope, a tow line), between the object it is on (a rod's tip, a bollard; the Rigidbody it is on or under, if any and not kinematic, is pulled too) and its **Body**, made fast at **End Offset**. Elastic as nylon is: stretched beyond its **Length** it pulls with E A x / L (Hooke's law), from its **Diameter** and **Stiffness**; slack, it pulls nothing. Bounded like the buoyancy: the pull is worked out with the stretch and speed at the end of the step against the inertia of what is on each end (their mass and turning at the points where it is made fast, from the Rigidbody's inertia tensor, and the other forces on them over the step: a Bounded Buoyancy Body's `LastAcceleration`, or gravity), so a stiff line on a light float neither shakes nor flings it, and a weight hanging still stretches it by exactly m g / k. Applied with `AddForceAtPosition`, after the bodies (`DefaultExecutionOrder(100)`; `BoundedBuoyancySimulation.Step` steps lines too). **Damping**, **Slip Tension** (a reel's drag: pulled harder, the line runs out), **Breaking Strength** and the `snapped` event; `PayOut()`, `SpringConstant()`, `Ends`; it reports `Tension`, `Stretch`, `Span` and `Broken`, and draws itself with a Line Renderer, hanging in a curve when slack and lying along the water. `BoundedBuoyancyLine.Active` lists the enabled lines. In the core: `Tether.Pull`, `Tether.SpringConstant`, `Tether.InverseMassAt` and `Tether.DampingCoefficient`.
- Fish: **Bounded Buoyancy Fish**, a Bounded Buoyancy Body that swims, its head toward +Z. Its swim bladder keeps it as dense as the water; its tail (a Bounded Buoyancy Thruster on a child object it makes) pushes it, falling to nothing at **Burst Speed**, against the water's drag; it steers itself with a torque against its own inertia and keeps upright. It wanders between **Min Depth** and **Max Depth**, comes to bait (its **Bait**, or else any Rigidbody with the new **Bounded Buoyancy Bait** component, which stands in for the Godot plugin's group), nibbles at it (each nibble a tug that dips a float), takes it and swims off with it, any line tied to the bait then tied to its **Mouth**. Struck (a pull over **Strike Pull**) while it has the bait, it is hooked; unstruck, it drops the bait and keeps away for **Wary Time**. Hooked, it runs from the angler, veering and diving; fighting tires it (**Endurance**), rest brings some strength back, and it throws the hook if the angler's line goes slack for **Slack Escape** seconds, or escapes if a line breaks. Events for each step of it; `State`, `Stamina`, `Effort`, `Line`, `MouthPosition`, `LinesToAngler()`; **Random Seed** for repeatable choices. A fish that cannot reach bait within 40 s gives up and keeps away for its Wary Time; one disabled or destroyed with the bait in its mouth lets go of it, the lines tied back where they were on the bait (or left free if the bait is gone); a copied fish keeps its one tail.
- Real water: a Bounded Buoyancy Water's density is now real water's, from its **Temperature**, **Salinity** and the pressure at each depth (UNESCO's equation of state, EOS-80: Millero and Poisson, 1981; Millero and others, 1980), with a thermocline down to a **Deep Temperature** and an optional halocline (**Deep Salinity Change**, **Thermocline Depth**). Fresh water at 15 °C, the default for a new water, is 999.1 kg/m³ (0.09% less than the 1000 before). **Fixed Density** uses **Density** as before (with an optional **Density Gradient**); setting `Density` from a script turns it on. Water saved with an earlier version loads with Fixed Density on, so existing scenes and prefabs keep their density (the component now has a hidden serialized version number, and data without it is migrated when it is loaded; see the README). `DensityAt(point)`, `WaterAt(depth)`, `IsLayered` and the static `SeawaterDensity(salinity, temperature, pressure)`. **API change:** the water's `density` field is now the `Density` property (the serialized data is unchanged).
- Layers: **Layers** on a Bounded Buoyancy Water (`BoundedBuoyancyLayer`: oil over water, brine, mud), each with its **Depth**, **Density**, **Density Gradient**, **Drag** and **Color**, drawn as tinted boxes (**Show Layers**); the top water has a **Drag** too. Bodies are weighed layer by layer (their shape cut at each boundary, each part weighed at its own centre), so they settle where their density matches, rest on boundaries partly in each layer, and are slowed in draggy layers; bobbing at a boundary sends out internal waves that settle them (the wave-making damping, with the jump in density across the boundary). Bodies report `WaterDensityHere` and `MediumDrag`. A fish's swim bladder matches the water where it is. In the core: `Seawater`, `WaterLayer`, `Stratum`, `WaterVolume.FixedDensity`, `Temperature`, `Salinity`, `DeepTemperature`, `DeepSalinityChange`, `ThermoclineDepth`, `DensityGradient`, `Drag`, `Layers`, `SurfaceDensity`, `IsLayered`, `WaterAt`, `Strata` and `DensityAt`, `WaterSample.Volume`, and `BodyCore3D.WaterDensityHere` and `MediumDrag`. A WaterVolume made in code keeps a fixed density unless told otherwise.
- Real air: bodies are in the standard atmosphere by default (the US Standard Atmosphere, 1976), which thins with height as real air does (1.225 kg/m³ at sea level at 15 °C, against the 1.2 everywhere before). **Bounded Buoyancy Air** on an object at sea level sets the day's **Temperature**, **Pressure** and **Humidity** (damp air is less dense). A body's **Fixed Air** uses its **Air Density** and **Air Half Height** as before; setting `AirDensity` or `AirHalfHeight` from a script turns it on, and bodies saved with an earlier version load with it on, keeping their air. A cannonball's speed of sound follows the air's temperature (340.3 m/s at 15 °C) unless its air is fixed. `BoundedBuoyancyAir.In(scene)`, `DensityIn`, `SoundSpeedIn`, `StandardDensity`, `StandardAir`. **API change:** the body's `airDensity` and `airHalfHeight` fields are now the `AirDensity` and `AirHalfHeight` properties (the serialized data is unchanged; Bounded Buoyancy Body 2D is as before). In the core: `Atmosphere`, `AirField`, `ThermalField` and `BodyCore3D.Air` and `AirAt`.
- Warm places: **Bounded Buoyancy Thermal**, a column (or ball) of warm or cold air with **Warming** (less dense air, by the gas law) and **Updraft** (rising air that carries bodies as wind does: `BoundedBuoyancyWind.TotalAt` includes it).
- Bounded Buoyancy Body has a `BeforeStep(dt)` hook for subclasses, called each physics step before the buoyancy (the fish swims from it), and its `OnDisable` is now protected virtual (a subclass that overrides it must call `base.OnDisable()`).
- Tests: B21 and B22 play-mode tests (UNESCO's values reached through the water component; fresh and sea water; pressure at depth; a ball bobbing about its own depth in stratified water at an internal wave's period; settling on a thermocline; an egg on brine; blocks in and under oil; a stone in mud; the standard atmosphere's tables; a body 1000 m up and on a hot damp day; a sealed balloon floating where the air is as dense as it is; a thermal's warm air and updraft, and a balloon climbing in it) and three edit-mode tests (EOS-80 against UNESCO's check values, layers and real water in the core, the standard atmosphere, damp air and a thermal); the .NET checks add the Godot plugin's B21 and B22, 36 of them, with the same results to the printed digits. The earlier play-mode tests fix their water at 1000 kg/m³ and their air (cannon shot at 1.2 kg/m³ and 343 m/s), as they are worked out for, and the earlier .NET checks are unchanged (the core keeps fixed water and air unless told otherwise).
- Tests: a B20 play-mode test (a line's stretch under a hanging weight against Hooke's law; lead under the water pulling with its weight less its buoyancy; a slack line, and a line that lets go of a weight thrown toward its post; a stiff wire on a light float; momentum kept between two free bodies, and the stretch within what their energy allows; a line slipping at its slip tension, and one breaking; a fish that neither sinks nor rises and cruises no faster than it means to; a stick float cocked by its shot to show its tip; a nibble dipping it; a fish dropping the bait unstruck, hooked when struck, tiring on a held line, and throwing the hook on a slack one) and an edit-mode test (a hanging weight stepped with the core's pull stretches the line by m g / k; slack, closing and slipping lines; the give at a point off the centre of mass); the .NET checks add the Godot plugin's B20 checks that need no engine, 19 of them, the line stepped after the bodies as the component is.

## [2.4.0] - 2026-10-14

- Flooding: **Bounded Buoyancy Compartment** on child objects of a body: boxes that fill with water through their **Holes** (orifice law) and drain through holes above the water, with **Pump Rate**, `AddHole(worldPoint, area)` and `PatchHoles()`. The water lies level as the body leans and is weight carried at its own centre, applied as weight plus its turning moment about the centre of mass, and in the inertia tensor as weight at its centre (worked out again when it changes by 1%, and back to the body's own once dry). Bodies report `FloodMass`. In the core, `Compartment3D` and `BodyCore3D.Compartments`, `FloodMass` and `FloodInertia`.
- Cannons: **Bounded Buoyancy Cannon** fires round shot from a 6- to 32-pounder (the shot's size and weight from Muller, 1768; 488 m/s), with the ship's velocity at the muzzle added and the recoil (the shot's momentum and the powder gas's) given to the ship at the muzzle, so a broadside heels it; reload time, scatter, a shot prefab if wanted, `Fire()` and the `fired` event.
- Round shot: **Bounded Buoyancy Cannonball** (a Bounded Buoyancy Body) for any fast ball: air drag that changes with its speed through the sound barrier (Miller and Bailey, 1979), skipping off the water at shallow angles (Birkhoff and others, 1944; the step worked through in short steps, the Rigidbody carried along their chord and its real velocity handed back the next step), continuous collision detection and a `hit` event; `Skips`, `ExitVelocity` and `Mach`. In the core: `SphereDrag`, `WaterImpact`, `BodyCore3D.TryStepBall`, `FastBallDrag`, `SpeedOfSound`, `Skips`, `ExitVelocity`, `BallCentre` and `Touches`. Bounded Buoyancy Body has `StepVelocity` and `TryFastStep` hooks for subclasses, and `ApplyMass` and `ApplyAngularDamping`.
- Damage: **Bounded Buoyancy Hull** on a ship, its planking (**Thickness**, and the wood's **Density** and **Strength**: oak by default). Shot that strikes it is resisted with σ + ρ v² on its cross-section (Poncelet's law of penetration; a 32-pounder goes 42 inches into solid oak from 400 yards, as Elvin, 1805, gives). Shot with the speed goes through, holing the nearest compartment (**Hole Size** times its cross-section), flies on across the ship (out of the engine's way: the cannonball ignores the ship's colliders while inside it) and out of the far side if it still has the speed, and otherwise lodges; the wood punched out flies off as floating splinters (**Splinters**, **Splinter Lifetime**, **Splinter Color**), and the ship takes the momentum the shot loses, less what the splinters carry off (`ThrowSplinters` returns it). Speeds are taken relative to the ship where the shot strikes it. The ship's colliders are taken as its outside, and as solid, those that touch or overlap along the shot's path as one (found with ray casts against the ship's own colliders); shot passes anything lighter than itself, such as splinters; a shot that comes out under the water is slowed by its drag for the rest of that step, and makes no splash. Shot that meets the sea on its way skips off it or plunges in first, so shot fired low holes a ship at and above its waterline. Glancing shot bounces off. Events `struck` and `holed`; the cannonball's `Penetration`, and its `hit` now also when it comes out through the far side. In the core: `Planking` (`Depth`, `SpeedAfter`, `StrikeSpeed`, `Glance`), and `BodyCore3D.MeetsWaterBefore` and `StepBallToWall` (the short steps through the water stopping where the ball reaches the planking: `WallStruck`, `WallTime`, `WallVelocity`), and `InTheWaterAlready`.
- Tests: a B19 play-mode test (shot into solid oak from 400 and 1000 yards; through a ship: two holes, one in each compartment, the momentum, the speed out of the far side, the sea in below the waterline only; a spent shot lodging; a ship of two boxes side by side shot through both; shot landing short of a ship's side at 4° and at 20°) and an edit-mode test (Poncelet's law against Elvin's depths); the .NET checks add the Godot plugin's B19 checks that need no engine, 8 of them.
- Tests: a B18 play-mode test (recoil momentum, a shot at 488 m/s stopped by a plank, an iron ball skipping at 4° and plunging at 9°) and two edit-mode tests (the fast ball's drag either side of the sound barrier, the water's push on a ball wholly under); the .NET checks add the Godot plugin's B18 checks that need no engine (ranges, critical angles, the steel ball against measurements, ricochet fire) and agree with it to the printed digits for the angles, the steel ball and the first graze, and to within one step's travel for the range in a vacuum and the time of flight.
- Tests: a B17 play-mode test (a list toward the damaged side, upright again and the inertia back once pumped dry) and two edit-mode tests (the free-surface shift, the orifice law); the .NET checks add the Godot plugin's B17, with the flood water's inertia in the turning, and agree with it to the printed digits except the sinking speed of the filled bowl (2.41 m/s against 2.43 in Godot Physics, which depends on how it leans as it goes down). `Sym3.FromPrincipal` and `Sym3 +` in the core.

## [2.3.0] - 2026-10-14

- Wave-making damping: a body bobbing at the surface sends out ripples that carry its energy away, so floats now settle after a few bobs, as real ones do. It acts on bobbing relative to the surface under the body (the Bounded Buoyancy Water's waves, or HDRP's), so riding waves is not held back, and on the speed at the start of each step, so the first frame after a release is unchanged. How much depends on the shape, from linear wave theory: a raft or a boat loses about 70% of each bob, a cube or a ball floating half under about 27%, a deep post 3%. The damping ratios were found exactly with the open-source solver Capytaine for 30 floating shapes and fitted with one curve (`reference/wave_damping.py` in the repository): in the simulation, 0.8 to 1.5 times the exact value for flat-bottomed bodies, about 0.5 to 0.7 times for balls and logs floating at least half out of the water, and within about 2% in 2D.
- The water's push in waves: water accelerating up and down in the waves now pushes on everything in it, (1 + C) ρ V a, a the water's upward acceleration at the body averaged over its footprint. So a small raft rises and falls as far as the water does instead of a little further (99% on waves 20 times its length, against 105% before; linear wave theory gives 100%), and a body under the water moves up and down with the water round it. Held to half of g, as real waves never accelerate faster. Bounded model only; still water is unchanged. HDRP gives heights only, so on HDRP water bodies are buoyed by the surface alone, as before; a link to another water system can supply the water's acceleration by overriding the new `BoundedBuoyancySurface.VerticalAccelerationAt` (and `VerticalAccelerationOver` to average a footprint in one go), and `BoundedBuoyancyWater.VerticalAccelerationAt` gives it for any point. In the core: `WaterSurface.VerticalAccelerationAt`, `WaveSet.VerticalAcceleration`, `ISurfaceMotion`, `WaveSet.MeanHeight`, `BodyCore3D.WaveAcceleration`, `BuoyancyShape3D.Size`, and the same in 2D.
- New setting **Wave Damping** on Bounded Buoyancy Body and Body 2D (1, the physics; 0 leaves it out). Scenes saved with an earlier version get 1. In the core: `BodyCore3D.WaveDamping`, `HeaveDamping`, `WaveDampingRatio`, `WaveDampingCoefficient` and `AtSurface`, and the same in `BodyCore2D`; `WaterSample.Owner` names the water a sample came from; `Immersion3D.Immerse` can return the height of the surface over the body, so the damping asks other water systems for nothing more.
- A body is damped only while it is at the surface, so one leaping out of the water or plunging in is damped only for the part of the step it is there; one teleported, moving into water at another height, or in water moved up or down at once, gets no kick from the jump in the surface; water rising or falling steadily carries a body as before; and one re-enabled starts afresh.
- Tests: a B15 play-mode test (floats settling, a raft still riding waves) and two edit-mode tests; the .NET checks add 47. The paper's benchmarks run with Wave Damping 0, so their numbers are unchanged.

## [2.2.0] - 2026-10-13

- Boats: **Bounded Buoyancy Foil** (keels, centreboards, rudders, fins, sails, wings) and **Bounded Buoyancy Thruster** (propellers, fans) components on child objects of a body. A foil is a flat plate whose force square to its faces is ½ ρ A q² C_N(α), with a lift slope for its aspect ratio (Helmbold) and a stall limit; a rudder turns with **Angle** (positive swings the trailing edge to +X, as in Godot), and a sail with **Auto Trim** swings itself away from the wind it feels. A thruster pushes along its +Z, less the faster it goes, giving a boat a top speed of its own. Both are bounded like the buoyancy: worked out with the motion at the end of the step against the inertia the body has at their point, so a foil stops the flow across it but never throws the body back, and a propeller never overshoots its speed.
- The engine-free core has them as `Foil3D`, `Thruster3D` and `Appendages.Apply`; the body applies them after its buoyancy each step, as a velocity change and an angular impulse.
- Demo sample: a BOATS scene, a motorboat to drive (W/S and A/D, or the sliders) and a dinghy that beats up into the wind in tacks on its own.
- Tests: three B14 play-mode tests (the bounded foil force; a keel, the rudder both ways and a propeller; a dinghy sailing upwind) and two edit-mode tests; the .NET checks add 52, including the Unity layout of a boat turning the same way as the Godot layout.
- No change to the buoyancy itself or to the paper's benchmarks.

## [2.1.1] - 2026-10-13

- Bounded Buoyancy HDRP Water: each body's heights are now found in one Burst job (HDRP's `WaterSimulationSearchJob`) instead of one managed search per vertex, so a detailed hull or a fleet costs little more than a sphere; and with **Use HDRP Current** (on by default) the Water Surface's own current carries the bodies (its speed in km/h along its orientation, the ripples current for a pool, turned with the surface's object), in place of the Bounded Buoyancy Water's Current.
- BoundedBuoyancySurface has an optional `FlowAt`, and the core an `ISurfaceFlow`, so any linked water system can supply its current.
- Checks: the search job compiled against the HDRP 14, 15, 16 and 17 API; a core check that a source's current carries a ball over the volume's own.

## [2.1.0] - 2026-10-13

- Other water systems: Bounded Buoyancy Water has a new **Surface Source**. Bodies then float on that system's surface, each vertex of their shape measured against it, with this package's added mass, righting and drag; the water still sets the size, density, current and splashes, and stops drawing its own surface.
- **Bounded Buoyancy HDRP Water**: the link to the Water System of the High Definition Render Pipeline. Add it to an unscaled object at the water's height, pick the Water Surface and turn on its Script Interactions; HDRP draws the water and Bounded Buoyancy does the physics. It is compiled only when HDRP 14 (Unity 2022.3) or newer is installed, and uses `FindWaterSurfaceHeight` in HDRP 14 and `ProjectPointOnWaterSurface` (with deformers, optionally) from HDRP 15.
- **BoundedBuoyancySurface**: a base class to link any other water system (override `HeightAt`, and `HeightsAt` for many points at once). In the engine-free core, `ISurfaceHeights`.
- Bodies near wavy water now check the surface over every part of them when the waves are short and steep compared with the body; before, a crest could reach a body that was counted as clear of the water. The benchmark results are unchanged.
- Tests: a new play-mode test (B13) and two edit-mode tests of another water system's surface; the .NET checks add five, and compile the HDRP link against the HDRP 14 and HDRP 17 API.

## [2.0.0] - 2026-10-11

First Unity release, with the features of Bounded Buoyancy 2.0.0 for Godot.

- Bounded Buoyancy Body for Rigidbody: the added mass of the submerged part in the inertia every physics step, so buoyancy drives no fall faster than g and no rise faster than g/C; momentum kept on entry; a Usual model for comparison.
- Any collider shape: box (scaled, even skewed), sphere, capsule and convex mesh, on the object or its children, or a closed buoyancy mesh. Buoyancy acts at the centre of the part under water, so bodies tilt, roll and right themselves. Centre of mass at the centre of the volume (with an offset for ballast), and inertia with its principal axes, worked out from the shape.
- Bounded Buoyancy Water: water volumes at any height, with waves, a current (slower along the banks), splashes, rings and a drawn surface that works in every render pipeline.
- Air: balloons and hot-air balloons, air that thins with height, and density changed while running.
- Bounded Buoyancy Wind: wind with gusts that travel downwind, limited to a box if wanted; sails that heel a boat.
- Water Entered and Water Exited events with the speed and the point on the surface.
- 2D: Bounded Buoyancy Body 2D, Water 2D and Wind 2D for Rigidbody2D, with circle, box, capsule and polygon colliders.
- Unity 2022.3 LTS and Unity 6 (linearVelocity, linearDamping and angularDamping in Unity 6).
- Play-mode tests of the paper's benchmarks in Unity's physics, edit-mode tests of the engine-free core, and a demo sample with eight 3D scenes and a 2D scene, opened from Tools > Bounded Buoyancy.
